Why This Burger?

The Big Mac debuted in 1967 at a McDonald's franchise in Uniontown, Pennsylvania, created by Jim Delligatti. It became a national menu item in 1968 and has since sold over 550 million annually in the United States alone.

What You'll Learn

  • Lesson 1 — Ingredients & Prep: Sourcing the right beef (80/20 grind), replicating the special sauce, prepping the three-part bun, and slicing lettuce to the proper 1/8-inch shred.
  • Lesson 2 — Technique & Assembly: Smashing 1.6 oz (45 g) patties on a 400 °F (204 °C) griddle for 90 seconds per side, toasting buns butter-side down, and stacking in the canonical order.
  • Lesson 3 — The Details That Matter: Why the middle bun ("club") gets sauce on both sides, how 2 slices of American cheese melt differently than one, and the 3:1 pickle-to-onion ratio that balances acidity.
