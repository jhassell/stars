Lesson 2: Technique & Assembly

Execution is where the clone becomes convincing. Follow the sequence exactly.

The Smash (2 patties, cooked sequentially)

  1. Griddle verify: Infrared thermometer reads 400 °F (204 °C) at surface. Cast iron or carbon steel; nonstick will not develop the crust.
  2. Place ball: One 1.6 oz (45 g) ball center of zone. No oil — the 20% fat renders instantly.
  3. Smash window: Within 3 seconds of contact, press with a stiff metal spatula + bacon press (or second pan) to 3.75 in (9.5 cm) diameter, matching the bun. Pressure: ~15 lb (6.8 kg) for 5 seconds. This creates the lace edge.
  4. Season: 0.3 g kosher salt + 0.1 g black pepper per patty, sprinkled from 12 in height for even distribution.
  5. Cook side 1: 90 seconds undisturbed. Maillard crust forms; fat renders into griddle.
  6. Flip: Thin metal spatula, confident single motion. Scrape fond.
  7. Cheese: Immediately lay 1 slice American cheese on each patty.
  8. Cook side 2: 90 seconds. Cheese melts to a translucent sheet; internal temp reaches ~160 °F (71 °C).
  9. Rest: Transfer to wire rack 30 seconds while toasting buns. Prevents steam-softened crust.

Bun Toast

  1. Butter all six cut faces (3 buns × 2 faces) with ~3 g softened unsalted butter each.
  2. Place cut-side down on griddle (still ~400 °F).
  3. Toast 30–45 seconds until deep golden, not brown.
  4. Remove; club bun gets sauce on both cut faces (top and bottom).

Canonical Stack Order (bottom to top)

  1. Bottom bun (heel), cut face up
  2. 1.5 tbsp (22 ml) special sauce
  3. 15 g shredded lettuce
  4. 0.2 oz (6 g) diced onion
  5. 3 pickle chips
  6. Patty 1 (cheese side up)
  7. Middle bun (club), cut face down — sauce already on this face
  8. 1.5 tbsp (22 ml) special sauce on club's top face
  9. 15 g shredded lettuce
  10. 0.2 oz (6 g) diced onion
  11. 3 pickle chips
  12. Patty 2 (cheese side up)
  13. Top bun (crown), cut face down — sauce already on this face

Total stack height: ~2.75 in (7 cm). Press gently once to seat; do not compress.

Why the Club Bun Gets Sauce Twice

The middle bun is the only component that touches two patties. Sauce on its bottom face lubricates the patty-to-bun interface; sauce on its top face prevents the upper lettuce from sliding off the smooth bun surface. This is the structural secret — skip it and the top half shears off on the first bite.
